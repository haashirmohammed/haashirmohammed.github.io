About Me

My name is Haashir Mohammed, and I am the author of this independent research project, completed for Independent Research G/T. Over the past year, I designed and wrote a meta-analysis investigating how artificial intelligence can be used to augment gene and stem cell therapies for Parkinson’s Disease.

I was drawn to this topic because it sits at the intersection of two things I find deeply compelling: the rapid progress of AI and the urgent, very human challenge of treating neurodegenerative disease. Conducting this research independently meant teaching myself how to read dense scientific literature, evaluate four peer-reviewed studies critically, and turn a broad curiosity into a focused, evidence-based argument—one that ultimately landed on cautious optimism: AI’s capability is real, but its readiness for the clinic is not yet here.

End-of-Year Reflections

Finishing this project feels like reaching the top of a climb I wasn’t sure I could make. When I started, the science of gene editing and stem cells felt impossibly dense, and the idea of layering AI on top of it seemed even more daunting. What I learned is that complexity becomes manageable when you break it into questions and answer them one at a time.

More than any single fact, this year taught me how to learn independently—how to be comfortable not knowing something yet, how to find trustworthy answers, and how to persevere when the work got hard. I’m proud that this research is genuinely my own, and I’m leaving the year more curious about science and medicine than when I began.

To anyone reading this who is considering their own independent research: start before you feel ready. The understanding comes from the work itself.
