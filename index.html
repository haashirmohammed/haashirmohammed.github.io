Man-Made Measures: Using AI to Augment Gene and Stem Cell Therapies to Treat Parkinson’s Disease

By Haashir Mohammed

View the Research Read the Final Paper

Background of the Issue

Parkinson’s Disease (PD) is a neurodegenerative disorder caused by the deterioration of dopamine-producing (dopaminergic) neurons in the brain, leading to symptoms such as bradykinesia, tremors, and rigidity. According to the Parkinson’s Foundation, about 90,000 people are diagnosed with PD in the United States each year. It is the fastest-growing neurological disease in the world, with global cases expected to reach 12 million by 2040. Since its first description in 1817, it still has no cure.

Two of the most promising experimental treatments are gene therapy (deliberately modifying genetic material to repair or replace faulty genes) and stem cell therapy (regenerating the lost dopaminergic neurons). Neither, however, has yet reached the consistency and reliability required to become a standard of care, facing real obstacles in precision, delivery, scaling, and standardization.

My paper is a meta-analysis examining how artificial intelligence can augment these therapies. Reviewing four key studies, I found that AI’s predictive power is already measurable, reaching 89% accuracy in predicting prime-editing outcomes and improving stem cell growth efficiency by 15–18%. But my central argument is one of cautious realism: it is too soon to bring AI into the clinic. Every result came from in silico, in vitro, or animal studies, never a human clinical trial, and the field is held back by a lack of large, standardized datasets, dataset bias, and the “black-box problem” (the difficulty of explaining how an AI reaches its conclusions). As I conclude: AI’s capability, unlike its timeframe, is not under question.

These are key resources behind my research, including sources I cited directly, ranging from an accessible talk on AI in medicine to the peer-reviewed studies my meta-analysis is built on.

  • Video: Parkinson’s Disease Explained An accessible overview of Parkinson’s Disease: its causes, symptoms, and progression.
  • Video: “How AI Can Heal Healthcare” — Edmund Jackson (TEDxNashville) An accessible talk on AI’s promise in medicine, cited in my paper (Jackson).
  • Harvard Medical School — Benefits of the Latest AI Technologies for Patients and Clinicians Background on how AI can support clinicians (Ellis, 2024).
  • Parkinson’s Foundation — Understanding Parkinson’s Source of the diagnosis and prevalence statistics used in my introduction.
  • Study: Predicting Prime Editing Efficiency and Product Purity by Deep Learning (Mathis et al., Nature Biotechnology) The PRIDICT prime-editing study, one of the four core papers I analyzed (89% accuracy).
  • Study: AI-Guided Design of Lipid Nanoparticles for Pulmonary Gene Therapy (Witten et al., Nature Biotechnology) A core study on using AI to design gene-delivery vehicles.
  • Study: AI-Driven Quality Monitoring and Control in Stem Cell Cultures (Singh et al., Biotechnology Journal) The stem cell quality-control review I analyzed (15–18% growth improvement).
  • Study: AI Powers Regenerative Medicine into the Predictive Realm (Garmany & Terzic, Regenerative Medicine) The fourth core study, on AI across regenerative medicine.
